The Complete Guide to Karate Kicks: Techniques and Training

Technique February 16, 2026 9 min read

Kicks are some of the most powerful techniques in karate, capable of delivering devastating strikes when executed with proper form and timing. This guide will walk you through the essential kicks every karateka should know, from basic front kicks to advanced spinning techniques.

1. Mae Geri (Front Kick)

The foundation of all karate kicks, mae geri is executed by raising the knee to the target height and extending the leg forward. The striking surface is the ball of the foot or the instep. Key points include keeping the chamber tight and snapping the kick back quickly after impact.

2. Yoko Geri Keage (Side Snap Kick)

This kick travels in a straight horizontal line to the side. The knee is raised to the side, then the lower leg is snapped out at the target. The snapping action gives this kick its speed, making it excellent for close-range encounters.

3. Yoko Geri Kekomi (Side Thrust Kick)

Unlike the snap kick, the kekomi is a thrusting motion where the foot pushes through the target. The heel is the striking surface. This kick delivers more power and is commonly used in sparring and self-defense situations.

4. Mawashi Geri (Roundhouse Kick)

The chamber knee is raised to the side, then the lower leg extends in a circular motion to strike with the instep or ball of the foot. This kick offers good reach and power while being difficult to read and counter.

5. Ushiro Geri (Back Kick)

One of the most powerful kicks in karate, the ushiro geri is delivered backward. The chamber involves bringing the knee up and to the side, then thrusting backward with the heel. This kick is particularly effective against charging opponents.

6. Gyaku Geri (Reverse Kick)

This powerful kick uses the opposite motion of mawashi geri, traveling in a more linear path. The hip rotates to generate significant power, making it a favorite for breaking boards and delivering knockout blows.

7. Mikazuki Geri (Hook Kick)

An advanced kick where the foot hooks over the target after making initial contact. This unusual trajectory makes it difficult to defend against. The technique requires exceptional flexibility and timing.

8. Ushiro Geri Mawashi (Spinning Back Kick)

This combination technique involves turning away from the target, spinning, and delivering a back kick. The full rotation generates tremendous power, making it one of the hardest kicks in karate to defend against.

Training Drills for Better Kicks

  • Practice kicks slowly in the air to focus on form and technique
  • Use the heavy bag to develop power and timing
  • Hold each kick for 3-5 seconds to build strength and balance
  • Work on flexibility exercises daily to increase kick height
  • Practice combinations to develop fluidity between techniques
  • Shadow spar to improve kick speed and reaction time

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Many beginners chamber their kicks too loosely, reducing both speed and power. Another common error is telegraphing kicks by dropping the shoulder or shifting weight before the technique. Focus on maintaining tight chambers and explosive hip rotation.

Conclusion

Developing powerful, accurate kicks requires consistent practice, proper technique, and patience. Start with the fundamental kicks and progressively work toward more advanced techniques. Remember that flexibility and core strength are essential for kick development, so incorporate conditioning exercises into your training routine.
